How to play

A private tracker for nail biting, skin picking and hair pulling: log an urge in two taps, see your patterns and grow a small world on good days.

Body-focused repetitive behaviors (BFRB) such as trichotillomania and dermatillomania are hard to notice while they happen. Seedle makes noticing quick: tap the behavior, then choose Resisted or Did it. Add a body area, intensity, trigger or note if you like, or skip them.

Two Ways To Count Progress

  • Reduce: every Did it is counted, and each day is compared with your own recent average. Fewer than usual is progress.
  • Strict streak: only a clean day counts, and one Did it resets the streak. Your longest streak stays saved.

Switch any time in Settings. Switching never deletes or rewrites your history.

Seeds And Plants

Each good day grows your world by one step. Resisting an urge earns a seed, and you choose what to plant. A hard stretch pauses growth, and nothing is ever taken away. No spins, no loot boxes, and nothing to buy for progress.

In The Moment

Long-press a behavior for a 60-second breathing exercise.

See What Changes

Daily, weekly and monthly charts compare this week with last week and show the hours when urges come most often. Your full history is kept, and you can correct or remove any entry.

Private By Design

  • No account, no sign-up, no email.
  • Your data stays on your phone. No ads.
  • Back up to a file and restore it on a new phone.
  • One daily reminder at the time you choose.

What Is Free

Tracking is free and unlimited: all three behaviors, every body area from fingers and cuticles to scalp and eyebrows, full history, charts, the world and backup. Premium adds trigger insights, world themes, more urge exercises, a home-screen widget and CSV export. The yearly plan starts with a one-week free trial for new subscribers, and a monthly plan is also available. You see the price before setup.

Use Seedle as a nail biting tracker, a skin picking tracker or a hair pulling tracker, or for all three at once. It is a self-help tool, not a medical device, and it does not diagnose or treat any condition.
